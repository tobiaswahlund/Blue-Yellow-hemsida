What Decides the Battle

What Your Support Becomes

Drone Systems

Reconnaissance, situational awareness, and countermeasures. Drones and complete drone systems account for 70 to 80 per cent of all support delivered. Over 12,000 systems have been delivered and the need remains substantial. The Vampire, DJI Mavic 3T, and fibre-guided Ptashka are representative examples.

Vehicles

Transport, evacuation, and logistics. Trucks, pick-up trucks, armoured ambulances, and passenger vehicles form a central part of on-site support. The Toyota Hilux and Nissan Navara are among the most widely deployed platforms.

Optics

Night vision equipment and thermal technology. Binoculars, thermal sights, and optical systems for observation and reconnaissance under adverse conditions. Pulsar, Infiray, and PVS-31 are representative examples.

Communications

Radio and communications systems. Starlink terminals and robust communications even under jamming and in exposed environments. The Motorola DP4801E is a recurring system in deliveries.

Protection

Personal protective equipment and emergency materiel. Helmets, body armour, and individual medical supplies determine who returns home and who does not.

Medical

Trauma care and acute medical treatment. Emergency surgery and other life-saving care at the zero line and in clinics in liberated and recaptured areas.

Blue/Yellow does not procure or supply weapons or ammunition. All support is defensive in nature, fully traceable, and signed off by the receiving unit.

What We Do

Direct support to those defending Europe

The Drone War is Decisive

Between 70 and 80 per cent of what we deliver consists of drones, countermeasures, and electronic systems. The majority are airborne, but the numbers are growing.

Electronic warfare disrupts navigation and severs the radio link between operator and drone. A model that functions one week may be rendered unusable the next. Continuous adaptation is a prerequisite for sustained effectiveness.

Two Tracks, One Mission

At the zero line, combat surgeons and anaesthesiologists participate in trauma care, with armoured ambulances retrieving the critically injured under active fire. In liberated communities, mobile clinics are established to serve elderly, sick, and children left without medical care, electricity, or water.

More than 3,000 patients have received medical care. All specialists work entirely without remuneration. Every patient at the clinic receives medication free of charge.

From krona to signed-for delivery

An active Ukrainian unit submits a written needs request.
Approximately 90 per cent of procurement takes place locally in Ukraine, close to the point of need. This reduces lead times and strengthens the country's own supply capacity.
Delivery goes directly to the unit, often within days.
The recipient signs for the delivery with documentation and photographic evidence.
Donors can follow the entire chain. What is financed can be traced, and what is delivered can be verified.
95%of every donated krona reaches its destination. A lean administration, local procurement, and volunteer effort at every stage make this level possible.

No empty promises. Only verified delivery

Blue/Yellow's strength and credibility rest not on what the organisation states it intends or aspires to do, but on what has actually been delivered to those defending Ukraine's freedom. Every operation must be traceable to a concrete delivery at the frontline.

Forbes ranks Blue/Yellow among the ten foremost Ukraine-focused organisations in the world. In 2022, the organisation was awarded the European Parliament's European medborgarpris.

Frequently Asked Questions
What proportion of funds reaches Ukraine and the frontline?

When you donate to Blue/Yellow Sweden, you can do so with full confidence. We keep costs minimal, and 95 per cent of every krona goes directly to our operational work.

The remaining 5 per cent covers necessary administration, quality control, and fundraising operations. Planning, coordination, insurance, and careful financial stewardship.

How is my donation used?

Our objective is always to support the defence forces and civilian population with swift and concrete operations. We procure and deliver equipment directly where the need exists.

The majority of support, 70 to 80 per cent, is directed toward defensive technology such as drone systems and countermeasures. The remainder goes to transport, medical care, and protective equipment.

Why should I become a monthly donor?

As a monthly donor, you contribute every day to securing sustained and resilient support for Ukraine's struggle for freedom. The war demands sustained effort.

Thanks to our monthly donors, we can act with immediacy when acute needs arise at the frontline. Giving via direct debit or recurring Swish is moreover tax-deductible.

Can I contribute in other ways?

Yes, there are several options. In addition to financial contributions, you may launch a personal fundraising campaign in support of Blue/Yellow Sweden on the occasion of a birthday or other event. You may spread awareness of our work through social media. Raising visibility and keeping support for Ukraine alive is an invaluable contribution in itself. If you represent a company, our strategic partnerships offer options including specific technical support or employee donation matching.

Do you accept clothing, equipment or other donations?

As a general principle, over 90 per cent of all materiel and equipment is procured directly in Ukraine or in the surrounding region. This strengthens local supply chains, reduces lead times, and supports Ukraine's own economic resilience.

The exception is vehicles and heavier materiel. We gratefully accept functioning vehicles, particularly four-wheel-drive pick-up trucks, transport vehicles, and trucks, as well as construction machinery such as excavators, wheel loaders, and tractors for fortification work and logistics. If you or your company wish to donate a vehicle or machine, please contact us directly at logistik@blue-yellow.se.

Can I change my monthly donation at any time?

You may pause, adjust, or cancel your monthly donation at any time. Please contact our donor services team at donera@blue-yellow.se for assistance with increasing or reducing your monthly amount, updating your bank or account details, pausing payments temporarily, or cancelling your monthly donation.

If you donate monthly via Swish, you can manage or cancel your recurring donation directly in the Swish application.

Note. To cancel a pending direct debit payment, we require notification no later than five banking days before the due date, ordinarily the 28th of each month.

About Us

Our mission

Blue/Yellow Sweden (org.nr 802552-3724) is a Swedish non-profit association and part of Blue/Yellow, Western Europe's largest civilian support organisation for Ukraine. Blue/Yellow was founded in Vilnius on 14 October 2014 by artist Jūratė Sližytė, Paulius Kijauskas and Swedish national Jonas Öhman. Blue/Yellow Sweden was established on 23 October 2025 as the organisation's first branch outside Lithuania. The organisation is religiously and politically independent. Our loyalty lies entirely with the Ukrainian people and their fundamental right to freedom, self-determination and peace within their internationally recognised borders.

Since its founding, the organisation has raised over 120 million euros and delivered support valued at over 1.3 billion SEK. This includes over 12,000 drone systems, 553 vehicles, 142,000 units of protective equipment, 293,000 units of medical supplies and 22,000 units of communication equipment. Blue/Yellow Medical has treated 3,644 patients and trained 68 Lithuanian doctors and medics since 2023. On average, 96.6 per cent of all funds raised have gone directly to support and support-related logistics during the period 2022 to 2025.

The organisation was awarded the European Citizen's Prize 2022 and has been ranked among Forbes' top ten most effective organisations for civilian support to Ukraine.

Blue/Yellow — from idea to Western Europe's largest support organisation for Ukraine

More than a decade at the frontline of Ukraine's survival

2014 — From an artist's idea to the battles at Donetsk airport

When Russia annexed Crimea and launched hostilities in Donbas in the spring of 2014, Ukraine faced a brutal collapse. The Ukrainian army was materially depleted and volunteers streamed to the front without basic provisions. In Lithuania, a small circle gathered around the artist Jūratė Sližytė, who formulated the name and core idea behind the civilian support movement Blue/Yellow. On 14 October 2014, the non-profit organisation was officially registered in Lithuania under the leadership of Paulius Kijauskas and Jonas Öhman.

The efforts started at the most elementary and personal level. The first transports contained sturdy boots, warm field jackets, simple helmets and ballistic vests. Jonas Öhman and the first co-workers drove the cars themselves all the way to Donbas. Öhman later described meeting the Ukrainian volunteers at the zero line as shattering: the soldiers wore an improbable mix of worn Soviet uniforms, British surplus garments, trainers and civilian knitted jumpers. They lacked everything, but refused to yield.

During the autumn of 2014, the organisation reached an early tactical milestone. Blue/Yellow managed to deliver advanced thermal sights directly to the Ukrainian units defending the ruins of Donetsk airport under desperate conditions. The equipment was immediately used to direct and correct artillery fire in darkness against attacking Russian forces, one of the very first documented cases where civilian-donated high technology had a directly decisive effect on the battlefield. During the first year, seven tonnes of humanitarian and tactical materiel were delivered directly to the combat zone.

2015 — Shelling at Debaltseve and the first drones

During 2015, the operations deepened and the methods became more systematic. The war escalated in intensity and the aid workers operated under constant mortal danger. During the battle for the railway junction at Debaltseve, one of the organisation's vehicles came under direct hostile mortar fire. Shrapnel tore up the ground around the car, but the conviction of the mission's necessity deepened rather than deterred, and deliveries continued without interruption.

The organisation understood early on that the military reality was fundamentally changing. Already at the beginning of 2015, Blue/Yellow delivered a civilian Phantom 3 quadrocopter to the volunteer battalion Skhidnyi Korpus in Kharkiv, one of the first steps towards what would soon become a whole new era of airborne field reconnaissance. Jonas Öhman was open about the unique nature of the work: the organisation did not deal with passive emergency aid at a safe distance, but functionally participated in the defence of Ukraine by equipping specific frontline units. In Sweden, the efforts attracted nationwide media attention when deliveries passed two million kronor in value, with a focus on optics, night vision, fragmentation protection and field reconnaissance.

2016 to 2019 — Technological shift and personal relationships in the shadow of the world's silence

Between 2016 and 2019, Ukraine faded from international headlines. The conflict was incorrectly described as frozen, while soldiers and civilians continued to die in the trenches every week. While international aid organisations packed up, Blue/Yellow remained at the zero line. With an annual fundraising volume between one hundred thousand and three hundred thousand euros, the operation developed into a technical pioneer.

In the summer of 2016, one of the first portable counter-drone systems was delivered to the Aidar battalion at the frontline, in the company of Lithuania's then foreign minister Linas Linkevičius. The organisation realised long before established military institutions that future battles would be decided by commercial drones and electronic warfare, and small civilian drones were transformed into invaluable eyes in the sky for Ukrainian artillery crews and reconnaissance groups.

During these years, relationships were established that cannot be bought for money. By personally driving materiel directly to unit commanders, volunteer battalions and the National Guard, a deep mutual trust was created. Field teams learned to know the units that managed resources with precision, the methods that worked in the field and how bureaucracy could be eliminated.

2020 to 2021 — Quiet preparation for the full-scale storm

By the beginning of 2021, Blue/Yellow had delivered equipment to a total value of around one million dollars since its founding. The figure was small compared to state defence budgets, but the effect was concentrated and surgical: the organisation supplied field units with exactly the materiel that official supply chains were unable to provide, from specially adapted night optics to encrypted radios and field medical consumables.

When Russian forces began gathering along Ukraine's borders in the autumn of 2021, there was no doubt within the organisation. Seven to eight years of uninterrupted presence at the front had given the field teams unique insight into the adversary's intentions, and while the world hoped for diplomatic solutions, Blue/Yellow prepared its networks for maximum load.

24 February 2022 — Immediate response to the full-scale invasion

When Russian forces rolled across the border towards Kyiv, Kharkiv and Kherson on 24 February 2022, the outside world was paralysed by shock. Many international organisations were forced to evacuate their staff and halt transports. Blue/Yellow was at this stage the only Lithuanian support organisation that already had established transport routes and verified contacts on the ground in Ukraine, and the long-term relationships from eight quiet years of war became the direct precondition for saving countless lives during the first critical hours of the invasion.

During the first day, the public donated one million euros. Within hours, vehicles were loaded with protective equipment, generators and optics for onward transport to the combat zones. During the year, operations grew exponentially: the organisation raised over 40.7 million euros in 2022 according to its own financial reporting, and the American magazine Forbes ranked Blue/Yellow among the world's ten most effective organisations for civilian support to Ukraine. The main focus remained firmly on non-lethal equipment that protected lives, saved civilians and gave defenders the technical prerequisites to hold their ground.

  • A total of over 120 million euros raised since founding through 2025, of which 57.8 million euros from private individuals

2023 — Trauma care in liberated zones and tactical training

The course of the war placed new demands on specialised field medicine. After Russian troops were driven back from the suburbs of Kyiv, medical teams travelled into severely scarred areas such as Bucha to assist residents whose hospitals had been looted and destroyed. These experiences laid the foundation for the official start of Blue/Yellow Medical during the second half of 2023.

Lithuanian doctors, maxillofacial surgeons, dentists and field nurses began carrying out regular missions directly adjacent to the front zone. In parallel, advanced training programmes for Ukrainian soldiers were established, where instructors with backgrounds in international defence alliances trained units in tactical trauma care under ongoing combat. Already in 2023, the organisation's medical teams treated 786 patients, a figure that rose to 1,485 in 2024.

2024 — The technological shift and ten years of resistance

In October 2024, the organisation celebrated ten years of continuous presence at the front. The celebration manifested itself at Lukiškių aikštė in Vilnius through a major benefit concert where over one hundred and fifty thousand euros were raised directly for the medical field missions. During this tenth operational year, Blue/Yellow raised over 20.1 million euros and delivered materiel including over 4,500 drones.

The dynamics of the war had now developed into an intensive technical systems war in which dominance of the electromagnetic spectrum determined survival. The organisation redirected resources to meet the threat from hostile drone swarms and artillery direction, by providing electronic warfare systems, spectrum analysers and secure frequencies that protected Ukrainian positions against detection.

2025 — The 100 million euro threshold and Swedish expansion

On 2 July 2025, the organisation announced that Lithuanian citizens and international donors had donated a total of over 100 million euros since the founding in 2014, a figure that by the end of the year had reached 120 million euros in accumulated capital. To broaden support and connect the Nordic and Baltic countries' civilian resources, Blue/Yellow Sweden was officially founded on 23 October 2025, the organisation's first formal branch outside Lithuania.

Drone systems and drone accessories now constituted a dominant share of the material support, with 5,096 delivered drones and over 5,300 accessories during 2025 alone. The Swedish branch immediately focused on mobilising the business community and establishing stable flows for purchasing technical equipment.

2026 — Sustained presence until full freedom

During the first six months of 2026, rearmament continued towards advanced drone and robotic technology, with deliveries of ground robots such as UGV TerMIT and VOLIA E for logistics and evacuation, and electronic warfare systems such as Hymo, REB Enigma Flagman and a range of electronic jammers to protect Ukrainian positions against hostile drone detection. Blue/Yellow continuously responds to new threats by supplying units with mobile reconnaissance resources, vehicles, counter-drone systems and field medical trauma kits, while the Swedish operation is in full swing and strengthens the Nordic capacity to assist Ukraine for a long time to come.

Our history is not about figures or organisation charts. It is about the people at the front who refuse to give up, and about the unwavering promise to stand by their side until the day when peace and territorial freedom again prevail throughout the country.

Our vision and mission

Our vision

A whole, sovereign and free Ukraine where every citizen lives in security and freedom within the country's internationally recognised borders.

We see a future where cities and villages recover, where fields are cleared of ammunition and where generations grow up without the sound of air-raid sirens or the threat of annihilation. This dream of freedom concerns not only a geographical nation, but forms the very core of a democratic Europe where international law and human dignity are never compromised under the pressure of violence. It is the same principle that was at stake when Blue/Yellow delivered its first thermal sights to the defenders of Donetsk airport in 2014, and it is the same principle that is at stake today, ten years and over 120 million euros in raised support later.

Our aspiration is a world where civil society's independent power is able to resist tyranny and where action always triumphs over hesitation. We refuse to accept a reality where exhaustion leads to oblivion, a risk we have seen materialise before, between 2015 and 2021, when international media largely turned their gaze away from Donbas while the fighting continued every day. Blue/Yellow stayed then, and that experience shapes our conviction today: support that ceases when attention drops is not support that saves lives in the long term. Through unyielding perseverance we keep alive the promise that those who stand at democracy's outermost border are never left alone, no matter how long the war lasts or how the climate of global solidarity shifts.

Our mission

To save human lives, alleviate suffering and strengthen defensive capability at the frontline by immediately delivering verified non-lethal material support and advanced field medicine directly to the individuals and units that need it most.

Our mission rests in the acute reality. We exist to plug the critical gaps where seconds and millimetres determine the difference between life and death. By removing bureaucratic obstacles, we turn compassion into concrete tools in the hands of those who risk everything. Our purpose is realised when a wounded soldier survives transport from the line of fire, when a field unit discovers a hostile attack in time thanks to the right reconnaissance equipment, and when civilians in liberated areas receive acute care through Blue/Yellow Medical's field missions, which since the start in 2023 have reached over 3,600 patients.

The mission is not static. It is continuously adapted to the reality of the front. When the war in 2014 demanded boots, jackets and ballistic vests, we delivered that. When the war in 2024 and 2025 came to be dominated by drones and electronic warfare, a development reflected in our drone deliveries increasing from 692 units in 2022 to over 5,000 units in 2025, we redirected resources at the same pace. This is the core of our mission: never to be behind the curve when the threat picture changes.

Principles and internal processes

The work is based on uncompromising field presence and a methodology refined through years of practical experience, step by step since the first deliveries in 2014.

Direct delivery without detours

We maintain our own logistics chains from fundraising all the way to handover at the front. No material is left in storage or gets stuck in protracted negotiations. Through direct contact with unit commanders and field doctors, relationships built up over more than a decade in many cases, the equipment reaches the recipient within days. Our own delivery statistics show that the time from confirmed need to completed handover normally lies between one and ten days, depending on the type of materiel and destination. This speed is not a coincidence but the result of a network that has been tested and refined since the first vehicles were personally driven to Donbas by Jonas Öhman and the organisation's earliest co-workers.

Needs-driven field logic

We deliver only what recipients explicitly request and need in the moment. When the threat picture in the air changes, we adapt. Our operations cover reconnaissance drones, electronic countermeasures, mobile communications solutions, field vehicles and complete trauma kits adapted to real combat situations. During the first half of 2026, this concretely meant deliveries of everything from advanced ground robots (UGVs) of the TerMIT and VOLIA E types for logistics and evacuation, to electronic warfare systems and spectrum analysers to protect Ukrainian positions against hostile drone detection. It is a constantly changing materiel list, driven not by what is easy to collect but by what the units themselves request.

Medical reach and training

Through Blue/Yellow Medical, we bring specialised care and training to areas where civilian healthcare has collapsed. Doctors and medical personnel carry out missions on the ground to treat civilian and military injuries, while soldiers are trained in advanced care under hostile fire to lower death rates in the field. In 2025, we formally trained 68 Lithuanian doctors and medics, of whom 33 in war surgery and field practice and 35 in the internationally recognised MARCH algorithm for trauma care in combat environments, an investment totalling over 900,000 training hours. These are not one-off efforts but the development of lasting competence that continues to save lives long after the actual training occasion has ended.

Transparency and precision

Every donated resource is registered, every transport is checked and every handover is documented with photo and receipt. We report how resources are managed and ensure that between ninety-five and ninety-seven per cent of funds go unabridged to field materiel, an average of 96.6 per cent during the period 2022 to 2025, audited year by year and reported openly. This level of transparency is not a marketing promise but an administrative principle that permeates the entire organisation, from the fundraising apparatus in Lithuania to the Swedish branch established in 2025.

Our long-term goals

  • Reduce time losses in supply chains to guarantee that acute materiel reaches frontline units within ten days from order, a standard we already largely achieve but which we continuously work to shorten further
  • Minimise mortality among the wounded at the zero line through broad distribution of field medical equipment and application of international trauma protocols, further developed on the more than 3,600 patients Blue/Yellow Medical has already treated
  • Maintain technical balance for Ukrainian units against hostile drones through continuous supply of modern electronic warfare resources, as the war is increasingly decided in the electromagnetic spectrum
  • Restore access to basic dental care and general medicine for the population in de-occupied and heavily affected areas, an effort that began in Bucha in 2022 and continues to grow
  • Serve as an unwavering voice for Ukraine's cause in the Nordic region until the day when the entire territory is liberated and peace again prevails, a task the Swedish branch has taken active responsibility for since 2025
About Us

Trust is earned one delivery at a time

The war against Ukraine did not begin in 2022. It began in 2014 with Russia's annexation of Crimea and the conflict in eastern Ukraine. Blue/Yellow stood alongside those who defended the country then and continues to do so today. Support has continued through every phase of the war and will continue all the way to victory.

2014

Founded in Vilnius by the Swede Jonas Öhman, in the same winter that Crimea is annexed and the war in Donbas begins.

Want to know more →
The spark in Vilnius

When the smoke settled over Crimea and the first shots fell in Donbas, it was clear that Europe had changed irrevocably. That same winter, Jonas Öhman and Paulius Kijauskas understood that traditional diplomacy and large-scale aid bureaucracy would be too slow for the reality facing soldiers in the trenches. The answer was to found Blue/Yellow in Vilnius — not as a passive fundraising foundation, but as a swift and precise tactical logistics operation.

The conflict rapidly faded from media attention. In the years leading up to the full-scale invasion, the question of Ukraine was relegated to the outermost periphery of international attention. The forgotten trench war in the east continued in silence. Political leaders turned away, and international aid organisations kept their distance due to the political sensitivity of the conflict. In the midst of this global indifference, Blue/Yellow refused to look away.

In the beginning there were no significant resources or official allocations. The co-founders and other key individuals drew on what they already owned. They committed everything to the cause.

Ukrainian defenders witnessed Blue/Yellow risking everything at a time when the rest of the world had forgotten them. From these early defining journeys grew the mutual relationship that endures to this day. As deliveries reached their destinations, donations began to grow. It was here, in the midst of the forgotten war in eastern Ukraine, that the foundation was laid for the network and transparent delivery model that defines the entire organisation today.

2022

The full-scale invasion. Eight years of experience are deployed at full scale from day one.

Want to know more →
The trial by fire and the scale-up

When the full-scale invasion commenced on 24 February 2022, the world awakened to a new reality. For Blue/Yellow, this was not a new situation, but the culmination of eight years of uninterrupted work. While other organisations desperately sought a way in, Blue/Yellow had already spent eight years in muddy trenches under artillery fire — eight years of near-total media silence about the conflict.

When the sudden surge of support for Ukraine gathered momentum across the world, Blue/Yellow became the natural choice for donors. The organisation had already spent eight years building exactly what was needed.

The war rapidly became a high-technology battlefield in which success depended on seeing first, understanding first, and responding fastest. During this critical early phase, the long-range Bayraktar TB2 drone became a global symbol of Ukrainian resistance. Blue/Yellow stepped into the international fundraising movement and organised a substantial campaign to finance the system. Direct dialogue enabled contact with the manufacturer, Baykar.

Following the successful collaboration, the manufacturer took the unusual step of donating the systems rather than weeks.

2025

Blue/Yellow Sweden is established at the end of the year, with initial operations outside Lithuania.

Want to know more →
The Swedish expansion

After years of intensive support directly from Lithuania, the organisation reached a new milestone at the end of 2025. To meet the growing long-term need and secure even greater resilience, Blue/Yellow Sweden was established — the first official branch outside Lithuania's borders. The move to Sweden was deeply strategic, aimed at creating a bridge between the Swedish people's desire to contribute and the unique knowledge of the frontline's immediate requirements.

The organisation rapidly outgrew its original framework. The demands of absolute transparency and professional logistics required a stable and permanent operational base.

Sweden thereby becomes a new central node in the support network. The establishment represents a commitment to the future. We have stood alongside Ukraine's defenders from the very beginning and will continue to do so with full commitment all the way to victory. This is a new chapter in Blue/Yellow's history — and it continues to be written.

Story

It all began with a drone

Kharkiv, early 2015. In a cold room not far from the front line, a small box is handed over to a volunteer unit. Inside is a Phantom 3, a simple camera drone. It is Blue/Yellow’s very first drone. No ceremony, no fanfare, no cameras documenting the moment. Just the handover of a new tool to people who need it. A tool that gives them eyes in the sky, helps them spot danger in time, and may one day save lives by seeing what lies ahead.

For a unit commander, it changes everything. Suddenly there are eyes in the sky. An ambush can be spotted before soldiers move into an area. A safer route can be found in the dark. Minutes are gained, and minutes are life.

Behind the handover lay months of work already. Blue/Yellow had been founded in Vilnius the year before by Swede Jonas Öhman together with Jūratė Sližytė and Paulius Kijauskas. The early years were chaotic. Requests poured in from every direction, and the hard part was knowing who could be trusted. Step by step, a network of reliable people took shape. Equipment was packed into ordinary cars and driven the long roads east, all the way to those who needed it.

That first drone became a beginning. What started with a single Phantom has grown into more than 12,000 drones. Reconnaissance platforms, heavy transport drones, and systems that protect against hostile technology. The same idea still carries it all: see first, act fastest, bring people home alive.

Twelve years later, the work continues every day. From a box in Kharkiv to one of Western Europe's largest support movements for Ukraine. The road has been long, and it continues all the way to victory.

No promises. Just delivery.

The figures below show Blue/Yellow's combined support between 2022 and 2025. The reporting is updated continuously throughout 2026. Everything is procured close to the front and delivered directly to the units that need it, and every delivery is signed for on site.

Drone capability is built on a coherent ecosystem of aerial platforms, sensors, signal equipment and protection against electronic threats. Reconnaissance drones are the units' eyes in the sky: light platforms such as the DJI Mavic 3T see in the dark with thermal cameras, while the heavy Bayraktar TB2 provides a broad situational picture over vast distances. At the front, Ukrainian workhorses such as Shmotka, Khortytsia and Kazhan are in service, while fixed-wing drones such as Shark, Leleka-100 and Fury comb areas deep behind enemy lines.

FPV drones are small, lightning-fast and cheap to deploy in large numbers, flown in real time with video goggles right at the front line. Protection against drones is built up from handheld systems such as the EDM4S SkyWiper and vehicle-based systems such as the Piranha AVD 360 and Bukovel-AD.

Want to know more about how the drone systems are used in the field?

Drone systems are used for reconnaissance, situational awareness, documentation, logistics, evacuation and the protection of both military and civilian structures. They are a central part of the modern information chain in a combat environment.

Reconnaissance and situational awareness

The DJI Mavic 3 and Mavic 3T are used for real-time surveillance, where thermal sensors make it possible to detect movement, vehicles and people in the dark and in difficult terrain.

Logistical aerial systems

The Vampire is used to transport medicine, communications equipment and spare parts to positions where ground transport is risky or impossible.

Strategic surveillance

The Bayraktar TB2 is used for long-range observation and contributes to regional situational understanding and early warning.

Power and operating environment

Spare batteries, charging systems and operator equipment ensure continuous operation in field environments without infrastructure.

Electronic warfare and protection

Systems such as the EDM4S SkyWiper and vehicle-based jamming systems are used to break hostile drone control and protect personnel and infrastructure.

A coherent operational whole

Drone systems have developed into a coherent system of sensors, communications, power and protection, where capability comes from the whole rather than from individual components.

On a battlefield, much more is decided than the fighting at the very front. At least as decisive is the ability to keep people, equipment and supplies moving, and to get the wounded out of the danger zone quickly. The Toyota Hilux, Mitsubishi L200 and Ford Ranger are used for last-mile logistics in difficult terrain, while reinforced SUVs such as the Toyota Land Cruiser and Nissan Patrol provide safer transport of personnel in high-risk areas. Ambulances take the wounded to the next level of care, while all-terrain quad bikes get through where ordinary vehicles cannot.

Bicycles have proved to be an exceptional means of transport near the front line. They are silent, cheap, need no fuel and are easy to maintain in the field, qualities that make them one of the most reliable options for the last and most sensitive stretch, where engine noise can give away a position and fuel supply cannot always be guaranteed.

Between 1 January and 31 May 2026, Blue/Yellow also delivered 136 UGVs (unmanned ground vehicles), while tests of unmanned surface vessels (USVs) have been under way to bring forward supplies and evacuate where crewed boats face great risk.

Modern protective equipment is not only about reducing the risk of injury; it must enable the soldier to carry out their task for long periods in a demanding environment. Modular plate carriers are expanded with MOLLE attachments and fitted with protective plates to NIJ Level IV or Ukrainian DSTU Level 6, which protect against large-calibre rounds and shrapnel. FAST and High-Cut helmets are fitted with rails for night-vision optics and hearing protection.

Boots must cope with cold, wet and long marches; uniforms must allow freedom of movement and withstand heavy wear in changing climates. Medical care and evacuation have also shaped the design: in the event of an injury, the equipment must be quick to handle so that care and onward transport are possible.

Want to know more about how the protective equipment is used in the field?

Protective equipment works as a coherent system for survival, mobility and endurance in the field.

Ballistic protection

Vests and plates reduce the risk of shrapnel and penetration injuries. Helmets protect the head and often serve as a platform for equipment.

Equipment for endurance

Uniforms, boots and carrying systems make long-term operations possible in varying terrain and climate.

Protection against noise and the environment

Hearing protection and gas masks reduce the risks from explosive noise levels and hazardous environments.

A coherent operational whole

The protection system forms an integrated structure where personal equipment directly affects the ability to operate and survive.

Thermal observation systems such as the Pulsar Helion, Pulsar Axion and ATN Mars detect movement in darkness, haze and smoke, while image-intensifying PVS-14 goggles make movement and medical care possible in total darkness. Motorola MOTOTRBO provides encrypted radio communication with AES-256 between units.

The war in Ukraine has shown that fibre optics have become increasingly important: fibre connections create stable links where radio communication can be jammed or intercepted, which is crucial for drone control and signals intelligence. When ground-based infrastructure is knocked out, Starlink V2 and Starlink Mini provide satellite-based connectivity for command, medical care and coordination.

In a war environment, the time from injury to treatment is decisive. C-A-T Gen 7 tourniquets quickly stop severe bleeding in line with TCCC standards, while QuikClot Combat Gauze packs deep wounds. The SAM Chest Seal and HyFin Vent seal penetrating chest injuries and reduce the risk of complications when air enters the chest cavity, a type of injury that quickly becomes life-threatening without the right dressing.

IFAK pouches are carried on the personal protective equipment so that emergency care is immediately at hand, and folding stretchers are used when terrain or the security situation makes ordinary transport impossible. Through these efforts, more than 3,600 people have received field care, around 2,000 of them civilians in liberated areas who had been without access to care for several years.
